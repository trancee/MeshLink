MeshLink routing simulation — stage F

Every number on this page is simulated. It comes from a simplified computer model of phones and Bluetooth links, calibrated against lab measurements (bearer bake-off, 2026-11). It is not a measurement on real devices. iPhone energy numbers use the Android coefficients and are bounds only.

Stage F adds the accepted design's remaining mechanisms to the stage E final v1 one at a time and checks the pre-registered criteria: A1 full catch-up with holding for others and tombstones (ADR 0014), A2 per-conversation routing tags (ADR 0016), A3 certificate lookups from a cold cache (ADR 0018), A4 large messages (ADR 0015). It then tests a lookup rate limit, replaces the power index with an absolute energy model and sweeps the scan schedule per density class.

0. Findings and run notes (simulated)

  • No arm passes every pre-registered criterion in every cell. Criteria 1, 2 and 4 hold nearly everywhere. The exceptions are listed below and all involve a handful of messages. Criterion 3 (busiest phone ≤ 1.25 × A0) fails for A3 in 8 of 10 cells (×1.28–×2.10) and for A4 in 6 (×1.33–×2.07). It also fails for A1 in 3 cells (×1.33–×1.34) and for A2 in 3 cells (×1.25–×1.34).
  • Criterion 3 is noisy at 5 seeds. Re-running the same arm with a different random stream (A3 in the main set vs A3 in the diagnostic set; A2L, which turned out to behave like A2, vs A2) moves the ratio by up to ±0.36, e.g. medium walking half iOS A3 ×1.90 vs ×1.54 and arrival Android ×1.65 vs ×1.91. A0 itself reproduces exactly. Single failures near 1.25 cannot be told apart from noise. The large A3/A4 increases (×1.5–×2.1) are consistent across reruns.
  • Where A3's extra load comes from. Every node with the certificate cached answers a lookup and stops forwarding it. A lookup therefore draws about 17 cache answers (one medium run: 5,623 cache answers for 321 lookups). Each answer goes back along its own reverse path at up to 1.35 KB post-quantum, and relays forward every reply (ADR 0018: the requester drops later replies). The diagnostic arm A3D/A4D, not pre-registered, lets relays forward only the first reply per salt. Within the same set, it lowers A3's ratio in 7 of the 8 medium/high cells (for example, high static half iOS ×1.24→×1.11 and medium walking Android ×2.07→×1.60) but brings it under 1.25 only in medium static and high static half iOS.
  • A2 (conversation tags): no material cost. Route requests per node-minute are within noise of A1, because with this traffic (a message about every 12 min per directed pair) the previous route has expired anyway. A2 failed criterion 3 in one cell where A1 passed: medium walking half iOS, ×1.254 vs ×1.23. That triggered A2L. A2L is a no-op in this traffic model: at every send the previous-epoch conversation route had already expired (one run: 0 old-tag uses, 677 misses), so A2L ≡ A2 up to the random stream. A2L passes that cell (×1.19) but fails high static Android (×1.41) and medium walking Android (×1.51), where A1 and A2 also fail.
  • Criterion 1 exceptions (small samples):
    • Medium walking Android, unicast from truly-low-density senders: 99.2% vs flood 100%. This is 1 lost message of 25 in seed 4, and A0 has it too.
    • Low static half iOS, A3/A4: "unicast medium 95%". This is 1 lost message of about 20 from medium-density senders inside a low cell.
  • Rate limit (Q5): without a limit the misbehaving phone costs honest phones 0.00/−0.02 pp delivery and raises the busiest honest phone by +10% (±22%) at medium and +16% (±3%) at high. Both are below the pre-registered thresholds (1 pp, 25%), so the rule says do not adopt RL1. RL1 also helps little against this attacker, because 1 lookup/s equals its sustained rate.
  • Scan schedules: the pre-registered rule (criteria 1–4 as stated) picks Balanced 4/2 for low with no faster Responsive schedule, and nothing for medium or high, because the final arm already fails criterion 3 at every schedule including the interim one. A conditional rule, which is a deviation documented on this page, counts only failures the schedule causes: failures also present at the interim schedule are ignored, and criterion 3 is replaced by "≤ 1.25 × the final arm's busiest phone at the interim schedule". It picks:
    • low: Balanced 4/4, Responsive 4/2;
    • medium: Balanced 4/16, Responsive 4/12;
    • high: Balanced 4/12, Responsive 4/8.
    Low is limited by newcomer join time: 4/8 gives ×1.45 and 4/16 gives ×2.35 the interim join p95. At medium and high, join time barely depends on the phone's own schedule (×1.00–1.03), because neighbors' scans find a newcomer. So yes: low should scan more than medium. The confirmation and background runs used these conditional picks.

Deviations, simplifications and cuts

  • Nothing cut: 5 seeds everywhere. Compute was about 21 min for all sets, plus a pilot and sanity checks of a few minutes, against a budget of about 90 min.
  • Firstness and sessions are per pair of incarnations. A session starts at the first delivered message and ends when either side churns. Each pair has one random phase for its conversation-tag epochs (5 min, accepted ±1 epoch). Route requests keep the sender's public tag as origin.
  • Claims match current-or-valid public tags within the message lifetime: about 7–8 epochs at 30 min, not ADR 0014's "at most 4 at 1 h". This matches more, not less. Tombstones are stored by copy holders and by nodes on the receipt's path. Relays do not cache lookup replies. Certificate caches survive churn. The link certificate exchange costs no bytes.
  • Energy counts transmitted and received bytes of all routing, control, link-control and handshake traffic at 1.25 µJ/B, plus 0.8 mJ/KiB of large-message bulk. iOS asleep time counts as online time with no scan. iOS figures use the Android coefficients and are bounds only.
  • Large messages: 2% of chat messages, selected by hash, log-uniform 16–128 KiB. They are excluded from the small-message criteria, and bulk bytes are excluded from criterion 3. Criterion 6 uses delivery excluding messages whose sender or destination left; raw delivery is shown next to it. n is 13–121 per cell and class over 5 seeds.
  • Join time is the p95 time to first link for joining and rejoining phones in the same cells; the interim reference is the main set. The schedule sweep reuses the main set's interim runs (same cell and seed).
  • The misbehaving phone is excluded from busiest-phone figures. Its lookups are not retried.

1. Criteria per cell and arm (30 min runs, 30 min lifetime, interim scan schedule)

Criteria: (1) unicast and broadcast delivery within a connected component ≥ 99% at low and medium, ≥ 95% at high, by the sender's true local density; at low density within 0.5 pp of flooding where flooding misses 99%; (2) idle control traffic, including catch-up summaries, claims and tombstones and the link layer's neighbor lists, ≤ 10% of link capacity; (3) the busiest phone's routing bytes sent ≤ 1.25 × A0's in the same cell (large-message bulk bytes excluded); (4) first message on ≤ 3 hops p95 ≤ 1 s classical / 1.5 s post-quantum with the certificate cached, ≤ 2 s / 3 s when a lookup was needed. Unicast p95 ≤ 1 s at medium/high is an aim, not a criterion. Values are means over seeds.
