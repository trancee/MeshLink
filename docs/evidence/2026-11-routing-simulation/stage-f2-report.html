MeshLink routing simulation — stage F2

Every number on this page is simulated. It comes from a simplified computer model of phones and Bluetooth links, calibrated against lab measurements (bearer bake-off, 2026-11). It is not a measurement on real devices. iPhone energy numbers use the Android coefficients and are bounds only.

Stage F2 re-runs stage F with added instrumentation and evaluates it under the owner's post-hoc amendments (decided after stage F's results): (a) criterion 3 is an absolute budget: the busiest phone sends ≤ 4,700 B/s of routing bytes (10% of the slowest measured Android link, 47 kB/s; large-message bulk excluded) and its energy is ≤ A0's busiest phone + 3 mW in the same cell and run; (b) a delivery or latency criterion is applied to a sub-population only if it has ≥ 100 messages pooled over the seeds (smaller ones are reported with n); (c) ADR 0018 amended: relays forward only the first lookup reply per request, so the final arm is A4D = A4 + first-reply-only; (d) no lookup rate limit, A2L not adopted. Other criteria as pre-registered for stage F. "Busiest phone" is the honest phone that sent the most non-bulk routing bytes in the run (summed over the arm's unicast and broadcast instances); its mW is that phone's.

0. Findings and run notes (simulated)

The owner made the amendments in this report after seeing stage F's results. They are post-hoc. All results below are SIMULATED.

  • A4D (final arm) passes the amended criteria 1–4 and 6 in 9 of 10 main cells. The exception is medium walking, Android, criterion 1. Unicast messages sent from a low-density position reach 99.20% against flooding's 100.00%. The pooled sample is n = 158, and the gap is one undelivered message in seed 4. The same failure occurs in every arm, A0 included (99.23%, n = 163), so none of the stage F mechanisms causes it.
  • Results for A1–A4 and A3D under the amended criteria. Every cell passes, with two exceptions:
    • the medium walking Android failure above, which every arm shares;
    • A2 in high static, Android, which fails criterion 3's energy limit: the busiest phone is +3.59 mW over A0.
    A2 adds no mechanism that should raise energy compared with A1, which is at −0.1 mW. A3 and A4D are at +1.6 and +0.3 mW. The A2 result is therefore noise from which phone ends up busiest.
  • Byte budget (4,700 B/s). The highest busiest-phone value is 4,211 B/s (A3, low static, Android; 90% of the budget). A4D peaks at 4,095 B/s (87%) in the same cell, and A0 already reaches 3,824 B/s (81%) there. At low density the busiest phone's bytes are almost all relayed unicast data (about 3.6 kB/s). Other cells are at 0.6–1.5 kB/s.
  • Effect of first-reply-only relaying (LD). Answers generated per lookup do not change: 17.6 medium static Android, 29 high static Android, 47 medium walking Android. LD cuts the replies that reach the requester from 17–43 per attempt to 2–3.7 at medium/high density. On the busiest phone, lookup-reply bytes fall from 100–509 B/s (A3) to 46–145 B/s (A3D). The lookup success rate and latency do not change.
  • Lookup answer distance. Measured from the requester to the node whose reply completed the lookup. In medium and high cells, p50 is 1–2 hops and p90 is 2–5 hops. 37–67% of lookups are answered within 1 hop and 68–95% within 2 hops. In low static cells, p50 is 2–3 hops and p90 is 6+. Only 38–41% are answered within 1 hop and 49–52% within 2 hops. About 30% of lookups there travel 6 or more hops, and lookup success is only 88.7–95.1%. The destination itself never produced the winning answer. Its neighbours already cache its certificate from the link exchange, so they answer first.
  • One-hour runs. A4D passes in 3 of 4 cells. High static, half iOS fails criterion 4. The mean of per-seed p95 for post-quantum first messages that needed a lookup is 11,945 ms (n = 122).
    • Seed 4 alone has p95 = 55.0 s (n = 25). Seeds 1, 2, 3 and 5 are 0.8–1.4 s.
    • Stage F's A4 has the same seed-4 value (55.0 s), so LD does not cause it. A handful of lookups in that seed needed a retry.
    • With about 25 messages per seed, the mean of per-seed p95 is sensitive to one seed's tail.
  • Scan schedules. The pre-registered rule is applied with criteria as amended:
    • Low: Balanced is 4/4 (the interim schedule) and Responsive is 4/2. Every slower schedule fails the join-time guard (×1.45, ×1.78 and ×2.35 for 4/8, 4/12 and 4/16). Low's Balanced is not 4/2.
    • Medium: the strict rule finds no schedule. Every medium schedule inherits the medium walking Android criterion-1 failure that A0 and the interim schedule share. By stage F's secondary rule, which ignores failures the final arm already has at the interim schedule, Balanced is 4/12 and Responsive is 4/8. 4/16 is ruled out only by criterion 3's energy limit in medium static, half iOS. That cell's busiest phone is +3.45 mW over A0 in the same run.
    • High: Balanced is 4/16, the slowest schedule tested, and Responsive is 4/12.
  • Low scans more than medium (duty 0.5 vs 0.25). Only at low density does join time depend on the class's own schedule.
    • A newcomer's density estimate is reset when it joins, so it scans on the low-class schedule until its first estimate.
    • Advertising is always on, so the newcomer finds its neighbours mostly by its own scanning.
    • As a result, the join-time guard barely constrains the medium and high schedules: worst join ratio ×0.99–1.03.
  • Confirmation on dynamic cells (Balanced 4/4 · 4/12 · 4/16 and Responsive 4/2 · 4/8 · 4/12). All pass except medium walking, Android criterion 1, the shared failure (99.20%). Join p95 relative to interim is ×0.99–1.04 for Balanced and ×0.82–0.88 for Responsive. Mean phone energy relative to interim:
    • Balanced: 16.5–21.9 mW against 24.2–34.2 mW;
    • Responsive: 20.5–26.6 mW.
  • Background cells (report only; Balanced; 50% of phones backgrounded). Unicast delivery is 99.87–99.92% and broadcast 100%. Join p95 is 13.2–17.1 s (median 4.2–5.2 s). Mean energy is 21.2–22.7 mW for foreground phones, 11.9–12.2 mW for backgrounded Android, and 7.5–8.3 mW for the half-iOS background mix (bounds only).

Deviations and caveats

  • Compute budget. Runs took 1,032 s: main2 550, long2 86, sweep2 282, conf2 79, bg2 35. With about 1 min of identity checks the total is about 18 min, against the ~15 min budget. Nothing was cut.
  • Different random streams for A1–A4 and A3D. f2-main runs A0 and A4D first, so their random streams match the other F2 sets. As a result, A1–A4 and A3D use different streams than in stage F, and their numbers differ slightly from stage F's. A0 is bit-identical to stage F.
  • Identity checks. The instrumentation does not change behaviour: a stage F row re-ran identically in every field except wall time. f2-main and f2-conf at the interim schedule are identical except for the event count and the global convTagsNew counter. That counter is shared across arms in a run, so do not read it per arm.
  • Interim point of the sweep. The sweep's interim point comes from f2-main. Criterion 3's energy limit in the sweep compares against A0 in the same run, at the same schedule.
  • Lookup hop distance. It counts the flood path the request travelled, so it is ≥ the shortest-path distance. Lookups count if they started in the measured window. The "answers generated" and "replies reaching the requester" counts include lookup retries.
  • Latency criteria. As in stage F, latency is judged on the mean over seeds of per-seed p95. Delivery is the mean over seeds. The sample-size rule (n ≥ 100) uses the pooled count.
  • Which phone is "busiest". It is the honest phone that sent the most non-bulk routing bytes, and its mW is that phone's own. The highest-mW phone is shown separately and can differ: in high static, the busiest-bytes phone draws about 25 mW while the highest-mW phone draws about 34 mW.
